# Synonym Best Practices

The following provides a list of best practices when creating synonyms.

- The system manages misspellings by default. To include words that shoppers might use, set up synonyms. These words differ from those specified in your catalog. You do not want to lose a sale because someone is looking for a "sofa" while your product is listed as a "couch". Capture a broad range of search terms by entering all the possible words that customers might use to find your products. To improve results, [set synonyms as one-way or two-way](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce/optimizer/merchandising/synonyms/add#step-2-define-the-synonym-by-type).

- Map brand names and abbreviations to their full names, for example "HP" to "Hewlett-Packard" and common product nicknames, for example "iPhone" to "Apple iPhone".

- Include industry-specific jargon and terms that shoppers might use interchangeably, for example "sneakers" and "running shoes".

- Regularly update the synonym list based on new search trends, product additions, and shopper behavior.

- Test the effectiveness of synonym mappings by analyzing search results and shopper feedback. To improve accuracy and relevance, refine mappings.

- Do not use stop words as they do not make synonyms more meaningful, but rather increase the amount of data that must be processed. Common English "stop words" are filtered out of synonyms, such as:

    a, an, and, are, as, at, be, but, by, for, if, in, into, is, it, no, not, of, on, or, such, that, the, their, then, there, these, they, this, to, was, will, with

- It is not necessary to define both the singular and plural forms of a word as a synonym. If you have a mixture of singular and plural terms in your catalog, Search finds the correct set of products. For example, if you use "pant" and a shopper searches for "pants", Search returns the correct products. The singular term "pant" is often used in the fashion industry and sometimes in retail, although the plural form "pants" is more commonly used in some areas. (The word "pant" technically refers to the part of a garment that covers one leg. You need a "pair of pants" to cover both legs.)

- Be consistent with the way terminology is used in your catalog. Keep in mind that regional usage differences exist, and sometimes industry differences.
